## Reading / Listening

The recent reading has been clustering around a theme: what it means to stay in the loop when AI is doing more of the work. "Don't be a meat proxy" made the case cleanly — relaying raw AI output without reading or synthesizing it just shifts cognitive load onto whoever receives it. Paolo Galeone's piece on engineering standards covered similar ground from a CI/CD angle, arguing for code ownership and discipline as the thing that keeps AI in amplifier territory.

The "lights-off software factory" piece from humanlayer was a useful counterweight to a lot of hype: the argument is that LLMs can't maintain codebase quality over time and that no amount of harness engineering changes that fundamental constraint.

On the infrastructure side, Netflix's writeup on in-house LLM serving was worth the read — the engine selection reasoning and batched constrained decoding details were the most useful parts.
