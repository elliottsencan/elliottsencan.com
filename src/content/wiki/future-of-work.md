The dominant question across these sources is not whether AI will change work but how that change distributes costs and benefits. [Falk and Tsoukalas](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-02t155432-cognitive-offloading-and-ai-how-reliance-on-llms-affects) frame this as a coordination failure: firms face competitive pressure to lay off workers before automation's productivity gains are confirmed, producing collectively worse outcomes even when no individual firm is acting irrationally. [Kevin Drum](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-28t074225-welcome-robot-overlords-please-dont-fire-us) adds the longer historical arc, arguing that intelligent machines, unlike past automation waves, will permanently displace entire labor categories rather than shift workers into new sectors.

Mollick's hands-on report with Claude 5 Fable [illustrates what that shift looks like in practice](/reading/2026-06/2026-06-09t190614-what-it-feels-like-to-work-with-mythos): the human role moves from doing to commissioning, with agents running multi-hour autonomous workflows. [Vogels](/reading/2026-06/2026-06-30t173037-a-return-to-two-pizza-culture) sees this compression as a process-level change — prototypes now arrive fast enough that they should precede the written spec. [Zaides](/reading/2026-07/2026-07-07t170607-the-software-engineering-war) captures the cultural split this creates between engineers who prioritize shipping and those who insist on quality, noting that where you stand often depends more on who's in the room than on principled disagreement.

Several sources push back against the assumption that AI can substitute for human expertise. [Cekrem](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-19t110710-the-tacit-dimension-why-your-best-engineers-cant-tell-you), drawing on Polanyi, argues that pattern recognition and design intuition are structurally inaccessible to AI and can only transmit through apprenticeship. [Gomes](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-14t223612-the-perils-of-ai-to-the-software-engineering-profession) warns that shipping AI-generated code without review causes skill atrophy and is incompatible with safety-critical systems. [Ghost in the Data](/reading/2026-06/2026-06-17t124905-the-competitive-moat-that-ai-cant-replicate) extends this to organizational trust: automating away human connection destroys loyalty that no personalization engine can rebuild.

Workplace structures themselves often preempt the humans they claim to support. [DHg](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-08t112608-your-onboarding-is-a-hazing-ritual-and-you-call-it-agile) shows how agile-branded onboarding rituals systematically set new hires up to fail while keeping dysfunction invisible to management. [Klepov](/reading/2026-06/2026-06-22t182141-the-systemic-decay-of-tech-hiring) traces how well-intentioned engineers produced hiring processes where error asymmetry and Goodhart's Law together select against the qualities they meant to find. [Malson](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-19t134831-finite-attention-why-burnout-isnt-your-fault-and-how) locates burnout in systems designed to maximize data output without accounting for human attention limits. [Nair](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-13t060018-why-senior-developers-fail-to-communicate-their-expertise) identifies a subtler dysfunction: senior engineers speak in complexity terms while organizations think in uncertainty terms, and the gap goes unaddressed because neither side names it.

[Gruhn](/reading/2026-08/2026-08-03t025839-dont-be-a-meat-proxy) distills a practical implication from several of these threads: the value a person adds is not in relaying AI output but in understanding it, and workers who skip that step shift cognitive burden onto whoever receives their work.
