Software engineering resists a clean definition because it names a practice, not a discipline. The sources collected here approach it from several directions at once: how code gets written, how systems get structured, how teams function, and what it means to be competent in the field when AI tools are changing the economics of all three.

On the craft level, recurring advice points toward legibility and restraint. [Go Monk](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-04t231343-ai-likes-deep-modules) argues that deep modules — small interfaces hiding complex implementations — reduce cognitive load for both human readers and LLMs. [Henrique Teixeira](/reading/2026-06/2026-06-04t073318-single-responsibility-the-distorted-principle) corrects the common misreading of the Single Responsibility Principle: it means cohesive grouping under one accountable concern, not atomizing everything into micro-classes. [Matt Smith](/reading/2026-07/2026-07-16t043206-i-stopped-destructuring-everything) makes a similar point at the syntax level, arguing that reflexive destructuring optimizes for writing at the expense of reading. [The unwritten laws collected by Anton Zaides](/reading/2026-06/2026-06-10t073045-the-unwritten-laws-of-software-engineering) — roll back before debugging, treat every dependency as a future outage — are the same craft sensibility applied to production operations.

System design questions run through multiple sources. [Ivan Velichko](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-04t231858-how-container-filesystem-works-building-a-docker-like) demonstrates how container isolation actually works at the Linux layer. [Colin Breck](/reading/2026-06/2026-06-30t185207-when-impressive-performance-gains-do-not-matter) argues that attention thresholds, discrete capacity increments, and pipeline backpressure explain why even large performance wins often fail to change outcomes. [Arthur Pastel's optimization of Rust's image-rs blur function](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-14t151252-5-faster-fastblur-in-image-rs) shows the other side: careful algorithmic work — replacing float arithmetic with integer accumulators and reciprocal multiplication — achieves a 5.9x speedup. These sit in tension without contradiction: systems thinking and low-level optimization both belong to the field.

Testing and verification appear as a persistent concern. [Currents](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-05t135218-designing-playwright-tests-that-survive-ui-refactors) argues that Playwright suites break during refactors because they couple to implementation details rather than semantic roles. [Emphere](/reading/2026-06/2026-06-11t024225-testing-a-security-tool-like-it-can-hurt-people) describes building deterministic test harnesses that prove a security tool fails loudly rather than silently overclaims. Daniel Stenberg [reviews curl's vulnerability history](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-02t094735-approaching-zero-bugs) and finds no measurable sign that AI-assisted static analysis is driving latent bug counts toward zero. Yaron Minsky [argues at Jane Street](/reading/2026-06/2026-06-15t021106-formal-methods-and-the-future-of-programming) that agentic coding makes formal verification newly cost-effective precisely because tests alone cannot provide the guarantees that generated code now demands.

The AI tooling debate splits the sources sharply. Lars Faye [argues that full agentic workflows accelerate skill atrophy](/reading/2026-04/2026-04-27t145041-agentic-coding-is-a-trap) and invert developer priorities toward speed over understanding. Pete Millspaugh [proposes a "Slow Mode" agent](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-19t193626-slow-mode) that keeps humans involved at every step to preserve learning. Abednego Gomes [treats vibe coding as professionally reckless](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-14t223612-the-perils-of-ai-to-the-software-engineering-profession) — categorically incompatible with safety-critical systems. [How To Test Frontend](/reading/2026-06/2026-06-22t185420-code-smells-when-you-get-ai-to-write-your-frontend-tests) documents 20+ recurring AI test smells: over-mocking, happy-path-only coverage, tests that validate a buggy implementation rather than intended behavior. Armin Ronacher [warns](/reading/2026-06/2026-06-23t161552-the-coming-loop) that harness loops amplify LLMs' worst tendencies, producing defensive and opaque code that may require machine participation to maintain. [HumanLayer's analysis](/reading/2026-07/2026-07-23t215330-humanlayeradvanced-context-engineering-for-coding-agents) goes further: lights-off software factories fail because LLMs cannot maintain codebase quality over time, a training problem that no harness engineering resolves. Against this, [Yusuf Aytas](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-22t091746-when-code-is-cheap-does-quality-still-matter) notes that AI lowers the cost of producing code but not the cost of owning it — taste and judgment still determine whether generated code becomes an asset or accelerated technical debt. Paolo Galeone [calls for strong CI/CD and code ownership](/reading/2026-08/2026-08-05t072544-use-your-brain-engineering-standards-in-the-age-of-llms) as the enforcement mechanism that keeps AI in an amplifier role.

Organizational and professional questions round out the picture. [The bottleneck was never the code](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-06t110728-the-bottleneck-was-never-the-code) — the real constraints are shared context, specification clarity, and management coherence. Tuhin Nair [identifies a communication gap](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-13t060018-why-senior-developers-fail-to-communicate-their-expertise) between senior engineers who think in complexity terms and business stakeholders who think in uncertainty terms. [cekrem](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-19t110710-the-tacit-dimension-why-your-best-engineers-cant-tell-you) draws on Polanyi to argue that the most valuable engineering expertise — pattern recognition, design intuition — is structurally inaccessible to AI and can only be transmitted through apprenticeship. [Fagner Brack](/reading/2026-04/2026-04-30t155134-learn-algorithms-for-interviews-forget-them-for-work) and [Vladimir Klepov](/reading/2026-06/2026-06-22t182141-the-systemic-decay-of-tech-hiring) approach hiring from different angles but agree that interview processes have drifted away from measuring what production engineering actually requires. [DHg's critique of onboarding](/reading/2026-05/2026-05-08t112608-your-onboarding-is-a-hazing-ritual-and-you-call-it-agile) makes the same point inside teams: practices disguised as agile process systematically set new engineers up to fail while making the dysfunction invisible upward.

What connects these threads is a consistent argument that software engineering is primarily a judgment practice — about what to build, how to structure it, what to verify, and what to delegate — and that the pressure to ship faster, whether through AI tools or organizational process, does not dissolve the need for that judgment. It relocates where and when it must be applied.
